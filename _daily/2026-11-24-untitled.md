Woke up today saying, "Today I am going to do a lot of stuff." When I looked out the window, it was raining. That was a sure sign that today I was going to do a lot of stuff. 

And so that's what I did. 

Mainly, I got productively side-tracked. I finished building out a stub project I had, which allows me to automatically add NZTCS (New Zealand Threat Classification System) statuses to Wikipedia species. This means that if a moth species is considered endangered in New Zealand, I can add that information and the little circles that say "Endangered" to the Wikipedia page. No one has automated this before, so it'll be very helpful when new reports come out from the  NZTCS. I've already added a few dozen to some moth species. I'm going slow because I didn't want to fully automate it, so instead I've built a bot that lets me manually check each entry before committing it under my name to Wikipedia. The code is [here](https://github.com/RichardLitt/nztcs-assessments/). 

I also passed 1000 edits on Wikipedia today. So, I guess I am now a member of the Cult of Science. I'll take it. 

I worked on my Wellington City-Nature-Challenge slides for iNaturalist, mostly by adding around ten slides that talk about how Wikipedia pages are correlated to more observations. This makes sense - when people know about a species, they can or have read about it, so they log it. For rare species, they don't know it exists, they don't look for it, they don't log it. I figure if we get Wikipedia pages for rarer species, we up the chance that people will find that species and log it on citizen science websites. Knowledge breeds knowledge. The slides are [here](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1cB5Voo5zZapz3HHiTPvVDQXx7-8Uk_n87af1C0ttBSE/edit?slide=id.h644b7b45288be8d3_0_562#slide=id.h644b7b45288be8d3_0_562). 

I also revamped the [Research Software Alliance](https://www.researchsoft.org/) website a bit, mostly by fixing issues with link checkers that have been bothering me for months, and then updating their blog so that it shows ORCID Ids for all authors. I closed around 75 stale issues on their GitHub repo. It wasn't paid work, but it's something I've been meaning to do, and it helps the team, who don't want to think about this stuff as much as I want to. 

I swam a kilometre. I know it was a kilometre, because it is a 50m pool, and I swam back and forth ten times. I figured out how to change my watch to have the pool length, and how to set it to track intervals better. The whole swim took me around 32~ minutes, which means that... I probably haven't swum a mile. I think my watch overestimated my distance last time. I'll need to go back. If anyone has hints for how to get goggles to not fog up, let me know. 

And I moved the SustainOSS Academic Map over to CURIOSS, finally. This map is really my project, although others have helped, some of whom I have paid through Sustain funds. Very few people contribute to it normally. I don't think anyone will mind, and it'll make updating the website easier. It is now at [https://curioss.org/academic-map](https://curioss.org/academic-map/). I've already integrated logos and the funding calls from CURIOSS into it. 

And I worked on JOSS, a bit, adding a new feature that checks whether a DOI is a versioned DOI or the concept DOI that always points to the latest version. This matters for JOSS papers, and editors are supposed to check, but I think it's an annoying thing to check manually and I think it'd be better to let computers do the work. 

I wrote some emails. I read some of *Dungeon Crawler Carl VI: The Eye of the Bedlam Bride*. I wandered around through the night market looking for dumplings, and settled on a kebab-like object that was delicious. 

I did a lot today. How I missed the rain!
