Today was long. I woke up at 1:30, and I've been awake since. I read for hours in bed. I sat alone and meditated in the dark halfway up a mountain for thirty minutes. I heard new owls call through the fog. I climbed high cliffs over shallow rivers, where tourists sat on bamboo boats, floating past gorges and temples veiled with pine trees. I laughed aloud at some birds I saw, serendipitously, just there, right there, where I knew they would be.

And I ended up in one of my favourite hotel rooms I have ever been in, which I had far too little time to enjoy. I mentioned grief in my first post. I am still sitting daily with my friend's death, last year. He would have loved this room, too. I felt such happiness and sadness entering it. I wish I could share the smell - cedar and tea - with you. I embraced the peace of this place, of the tree outside, a Plumbeous Redstart right beneath my window in a stream, for five minutes in the grey afternoon. 

Before the pressures of small-town hospitality forced me out, and my anxiety had me overpay and overpromise. Now it's almost not quite eight. I'm waking up at 5:00 to go birding with a local guide. I hope that's enough sleep. We'll find out tomorrow. 

No meaningful output today but this. But I was alive today.

P.S. I've made an automatic script to start these posts, but part of that is coming up with a title. Today I blanked. "Temples?" "Sunk costs?" "23 lifers?" Something else. As tired as I am, I realized my process was wrong. It makes the title direct a piece. It shouldn't. The piece should narrow down possible titles. "Name it afterwards" is as good a title as any, for this one.
