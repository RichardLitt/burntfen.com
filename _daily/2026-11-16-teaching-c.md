Teaching C is hard for three reasons.

First, I previously did not know C. I've been learning it for this course. Luckily,  years of coding in Python and JavaScript has meant that I have picked up a lot of basics when it comes to coding, and learning a new language was not as hard as I expected.

Second, it's hard to figure out the course syllabus, when I only have the slides from previous years. Some of those slides are very short. Today's was only around 7 slides long, for a concept I could have taught in two or three minutes. So, I need to improvise. I spend a lot of time thinking about how best to edit the slides, how to make the matter more interesting, and how to get examples written which I can live code so I can show my students what C does and how it works. I am using LLMs to help me with this, largely because C courses are exactly the sort of thing there are thousands of tutorials on the web for, so the hallucinations are much less bad than, say, relative hair density of extinct giant sloths and how that relates to extinct vampire bat bite patterns (speaking from experience, here). 

Third, I don't get any feedback from my students. Very occasionally I'll get a head nod. Once I got someone saying "Null", which was itself ironic. And I've had one student ask me questions on WeChat, the talk app of choice in China. But that's it. 75 students, most of them with their heads down on their phones. I suspect my English is too complicated for most, but also that they read the slides and just ignore the course. 

Altogether, it means that my 1.5 hour lectures are difficult experiences. That's a long time to talk in front of a room on something you mostly know, trying to go as slow as possible so you don't run out of slides.

 I'm a curious teacher - if I am up on stage and I have a thought about something, I'll say it - and then I'll look stupid, because I won't know the answer. I can't look it up. So I try to know as much as I can before hand, so that that doesn't happen. I also have to know every bit of code I have prewritten, or else I look like an ass who uses LLMs and can't be trusted (source agian: experience). 

Today, I felt like I turned a corner. I woke up at 3:00am to get my slides ready, and worked on them for the next seven hours, more or less. It took that long to understand everything, make more slides, make examples, and feel like I had a grasp on the subject. And it worked. I felt like I stumbled less, knew the content more, and for once the bell rang and I was surprised I had talked for 45 minutes. 

I'm happy about that. I spent the rest of the day resting, because I realized my trip to Hangzhou took a lot out of me. I'm glad to be back to routines. 

8:00pm. Time to go to bed and wake up early for the good internet.
