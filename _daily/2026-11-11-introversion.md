Tonight I find myself in a lovely resort-style hotel, with bamboo gardens, waterfalls and koi, and a small tea plantation in the back. I walked to the cascading waterfall restaurant at around six, looking for a quiet place for dinner, and instead found a room filled with families and couples, some of whom were blasting videos on their phones while they ate, without looking at each other. After a three hour train ride where people were doing the same thing, I'd had enough. I retreated to my room to feast on a banana and some cherry tomatoes, and a self-advertised "excellent and nutricious soup" in a thermos. 

Normally, I would be out and going to a restaurant, walking through a strange city, trying new foods. But on this trip, I find myself retreating more and more into myself. After teaching especially (and today was a teaching day), I have no desire to see other people or eat in a crowded restaurant. I don't want music or audiobooks; I just want to sit, read, and perhaps look out the window. 

It's not a bad thing. I like being in bed by 7:00. (I was able to make the European [CURIOSS](https://curioss.org) call, too, because I wasn't stuck eating somewhere else.) But it's different, for me. And I notice it.

Besides that, today was a lovely day. The train ride north was fascinating - thousands of high rises, endless houses with small garden plots between them. Whatever nature used to be in the lowlands has been relegated to small, disturbed outcroppings. Occasional very rare wetlands on the rivers, but mostly just building after building. It was the BOSWASH corridor on steroids. 

And class today was good - for once, I had slides left over at the end. I think I talked slowly enough. I've learned a new skill. Go me. 

Right. Now I am going to go owling for a short bit, and hopefully get up early for the dawn chorus. Tomorrow is another light travel day – while I'm here, I might as well see some things. I'll get a few hours of work in, I hope.
