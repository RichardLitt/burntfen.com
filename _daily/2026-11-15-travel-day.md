I made it back to Xiamen. 

Today was the second day of the conference, the day some of the VIPs normally skip. I went in for the morning, and I was glad I did. I made a few more connections. Mostly, I was proud of myself for being as kind as I could be to the kid who sent me on a wild goose dinner chase last night. I didn't tell him. What would be the point? I think about Ben Franklin, who had a list of rotating virtues to practice every fortnight. Some days I manage to get good work done. Some days I manage to write something worth reading. Some days I see a good bird or climb a mountain. Today, I practiced grace. 

Later, at the train station, I was stressed about making my own train, although I had 45 minutes to kill. I didn't want people to pass me in the line. I used my larger body to block passage, and told people not to cut. The woman who had tried to past looked distraught and complained in Mandarin about the foreigner who wouldn't het her through. Another person in line, in front of me, asked me to just let the woman and her daughter pass. I said we all had trains to get to, and I said if they can go, I should be able to, too, because I also had a train to catch. He said sure. So I moved up. A few seconds later, the train official said I was in the wrong line - foreigners with only passports needed to go to a different one. I stomped off.

The next few hours were miserable. It was hard to forgive myself for being contemptible, hard to process away the adrenaline and shame. I had had too much ego invested in my own stress, and then too much ego invested in my own misery. Today, I practiced grace. That doesn't mean I was successful. I learn so slowly. 

Now I'm home again. Going to wake up early to work on my slides, and hopefully have more empathy tomorrow.
