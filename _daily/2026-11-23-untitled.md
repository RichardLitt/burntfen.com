I spent the weekend walking. I walked for around six hours through the Xiamen Botanical Gardens, which are extensive. Once you leave the manicured areas, where piano music blasts from speakers as dozens and dozens of tourists walk around, the general feel is dry and Mediterranean. Lots of rocky outcroppings and pine trees, and various types of acacias. I saw a snake. I found some new lifer birds. I had a grand time.

After the gardens, near Xiamen University, I found a cat cafe / bar that had a vibe I loved as a student - lots of random plants and books, good coffee, nice music. It felt like an art piece, which was delicious after so many cut-and-paste shops everywhere. 

I walked on the beach and boardwalk for a couple of miles. I couldn't count the amount of wedding dresses on the rocks, being photographed against the sea. Thousands of people on the sand taking selfies, and not a single swimsuit. A couple Blue Rock-thrushes. A long chat with little understanding with another birder, a 60 year old man in a wheelchair with a long camera lens. Birders know birders immediately. 

Sunday, I walked up Tianma Mountain, the one next to my university, and replaced my nocturnal flight call recorder I left up there weeks ago. I somehow forgot my binoculars in my room, but I had a lovely long walk, stopping often for ten or twenty minutes to sit and listen. I found some good new birds. I saw another snake. 

Walking off the mountain by a very unused route, I walked into a family's garden plot and past their home. They were having tea outside. A 14 year old girl asked me how old I was - her first question. After being invited to have tea, I ended up chatting to her and her brother and sister (cousins?) for half an hour. The parents all left, and we just passed the phone around. The boy was so proud of himself for being in a robotics fair, but he kept grabbing the phone from his sister. I thought about moderating, and let it be. 

"You're our free English teacher," she translated. Yep. 

Today, I worked. There was a big inspection today, so we printed out work sheets for all 75 students, and let them spend 25 minutes going over it before I explained the answer. My coteacher spoke to them all in Chinese, and I believe he said that today was not a good day for being on their phones, because for once, almost every head was on me. I'm not sure I liked it any better - feedback, as always, was minimal. 

Two students came and asked me questions; one student asked really good questions, asking how to learn C. I told her later on Wechat to find something she finds interesting about C and learn all about that, and the basics will come through that. She seemed excited to have feedback and encouragement. It takes so little spark to make people excited here – just something different. 

In other news, there are rats in the gym. I've seen two of them, and more of their droppings. On the other hand, there are rats in the gym - me and the other 20 dudes. I feel a camaraderie I haven't felt before. We're all nerds together, putting in the work to bulk up and stay fit. Some of them could do more leg exercises - shoulders too broad for doorways, but they'll topple in a stiff wind.
