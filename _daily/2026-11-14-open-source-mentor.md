Attended the Chinese Open Source Conference today, COSCon in Hangzhou. Hundreds of people. State officials. Giant printed logos everywhere.

I won an award. I stood on stage, on a blue dot stamped "#11" and held a diploma-like blue folder with the phrase "Open Source Mentor" in it, unsure where to put my hands. I made some new friends. I sat on a panel, between GitHub and Kong, answered some questions on the future of OSS, and succeeded in not getting too political about it. I ate lunch outside alone. I saw an Oriental Magpie. I started _Dungeon Crawler Carl_ #3.

Then I facilitated and spoke at a birds of a feather (read as: an unconference talk and discuss session, my most common type) on academic open source. It went on for three hours, and devolved into life advice for around a dozen students of international business from Shanghai University. Selfies were taken. All of the students gathered behind me while I sat on a couch with two of their professors. I held their class flag. I wasn't sure how best to hold my hands again. 

One of the helpers told me where dinner was. The direction was kind, but inaccurate. I took a taxi to the wrong hotel, and returned an hour later, having missed most of dinner, which was downstairs the whole time. I ate some cold leftovers in my room. I read more of _Dungeon Crawler Carl_ #3.

Success is learning when to go to bed. It's also teaching engaged students, offering strategies on how they could best find work in their careers, life advice on confidence and appreciating their own skills, and then seeing them excited about open source and wanting to learn and share and fight the AI gloom machine, too.

The award continued, "in recognition of your dedication to championing open-source principles and nurturing innovative talent, and your contributions to the development of the AI open-source ecosystem through your expertise and efforts."

A good day.
